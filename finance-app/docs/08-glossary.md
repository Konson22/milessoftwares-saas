# 8. Words explained

| Word | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| **Account** | A category that amounts are recorded under, such as "Rent", "Sales" or "Bank". |
| **Chart of accounts** | The full list of your accounts. |
| **Ledger** | The complete record of every amount in your accounts. All reports come from it. |
| **Journal** | One entry in the ledger. Most are created for you; you write some yourself for adjustments. |
| **Debit and credit** | The two sides of every entry: where money went and where it came from. They always add up to the same amount, so the accounts balance. |
| **Reverse** | Cancel a saved entry with an opposite one, instead of deleting it. |
| **Invoice** | A document asking a customer to pay you. |
| **Bill** | A document from a supplier asking you to pay them. |
| **Credit note** | A document that reduces what is owed, for example after a return. |
| **Receivables** | Money customers owe you. |
| **Payables** | Money you owe suppliers. |
| **Payment request** | A request to pay someone, which is checked, approved and then paid by different people. |
| **Approval limit** | The amount from which payments need approval. |
| **Reconciliation** | Comparing your bank statement with your records to make sure they agree. |
| **Petty cash** | A small amount of cash for small expenses, kept by one person (the **custodian**). |
| **Float** | The fixed amount a petty cash fund is topped back up to. |
| **Voucher** | A record of one petty cash payment, with its receipt. |
| **Cost centre** | A department or branch that you want to report on separately. |
| **Project** | A piece of work, often donor-funded, that you want to report on separately. |
| **Budget against actual** | Comparing what you planned to earn and spend with what really happened. |
| **Fixed asset** | Something you keep for years, such as a vehicle, computer or furniture. |
| **Depreciation** | The value a fixed asset loses each month as it gets older. |
| **Exchange rate** | How much of your main currency one unit of another currency is worth on a day. |
| **Revaluation** | Updating the value of foreign-currency balances to today's exchange rate. |
| **Opening balances** | What each account held on the day you started using Tatook Finance. |
| **Financial year** | Your 12-month accounting year, for example January to December. |
| **Lock a month** | Make a finished month read-only, so nobody can change its figures. |
| **Close the year** | Finish the year and carry its profit or loss into the balance sheet. |
| **Profit and loss** | A report of income minus spending for a period. |
| **Balance sheet** | A report of what you own and what you owe on a given day. |
| **Cash flow** | A report of where cash came from and where it went. |
| **Trial balance** | A list of every account's balance, used to check that everything adds up. |
| **Tax return** | A summary of tax collected and paid, and what is owed to the tax office. |
| **Audit trail** | The record of who did what, and when. Nobody can edit it. |
| **Separation of duties** | Making sure different people ask for, approve and make a payment, so no one person controls money alone. |
