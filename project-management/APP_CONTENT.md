# Projects

Projects, spending, procurement and books for your organization.

Projects gives an organization its projects, spending, procurement and accounting in one workspace, and a shared record of each grant with those who fund it or receive it. It is made by Miles Global Softwares.

An organization can run its own work with no donor. When one organization funds another, a grant is what connects them. The organization that delivers the work chooses, grant by grant, what the donor sees.

## One line

Run your organization. Share what you choose.

## Who it is for

Built for the people on either side of a grant: whoever funds the work, whoever delivers it, and the finance and project teams who keep it honest.

### For those who fund the work

One dashboard across every organization you fund, showing what they share and what is waiting for you.

- Invite beneficiaries and implementing partners
- Follow grants, payments and funded projects
- Accept reports or ask for a revision
- Write to the organization on each grant

Any organization can turn this on. Your own work and records do not change.

### For running your organization

The whole running of the organization: projects, budgets, spending, procurement, grants and the books.

- Plan projects and run the work
- Approve spending, with duties kept apart
- Buy through request, quotes and order
- Keep double-entry books in the same place

Set it up yourself. No donor needed. A donor’s invitation also works, and you can connect later.

### For finance teams

Budgets someone else approves, spending checked before it is committed, and double-entry books that post as payments are made.

- Committed kept apart from spent, by category
- Spending held within the grant’s allocation to the project
- Reconciliation, tax returns and fixed assets
- Statements, and budget against actual

### For project teams

See the work on a board, a list or a timeline, know what is due, and raise a purchase or expense without leaving the project.

- Tasks numbered to their project
- A My work page and a weekly timesheet
- Allocation that shows who is overbooked
- A health reading from schedule and spend

It runs in a browser and needs a connection. Offline field collection is not built.

## What the product does

Each organization has its own workspace, members and records, and runs the whole job there: the work, the money and the books.

### Partnerships

An organization that funds others invites them. Each keeps its own workspace, members and records. Nothing connects until the invited organization’s administrator accepts.

### Grants

Award, amendments, allocations, payments and reports, with sharing chosen for each grant.

- Funder directory
- Opportunity, application, award and close
- Changes by numbered amendment only
- Allocations across projects and currencies
- Payment schedules
- Reports owed, with documents
- Sharing switches for each grant
- Portal access for a funder’s contacts

Before the award, the amount is the request and can be edited. Afterwards, the amount and end date change only by a numbered amendment. Amendments are never edited or deleted. A grant closes only when no report is owed.

### Projects and tasks

Planning the work and seeing how it stands.

- Projects with a code, manager, dates and status
- Portfolio with a health reading, worst first
- Spaces, folders and lists
- Board, list and timeline
- Subtasks, checklists and dependencies
- Statuses for each list
- Comments, mentions and attachments
- Milestones and a risk register
- Team allocation and overbooking
- Time logs and a weekly timesheet
- Programmes: groups of projects under one aim
- Deliverables, and change requests that cannot be edited after they are raised

A project’s code numbers its tasks, so `WASH` produces `WASH-12`. A task cannot be marked done while a task it waits on is still open. Dependencies that would make a circle are refused.

Health, for each project:

- Off track: past its due date, or over budget.
- At risk: progress more than the allowed slack behind the time elapsed, any overdue tasks, or spend more than that slack ahead of progress. The slack defaults to 20 points and the organization can change it.
- Progress is weighted by estimate.

### Budgets and spending

From the approved budget to the payment, with a different person at each step.

- Budget versions by cost category
- Approval by someone other than the drafter
- Budget, committed, spent and available
- Requests checked against budget and grant
- Approval bands of one to three people
- Receipts and quotes on the request
- One place for everything awaiting approval
- Funding by grant on each project
- Commitments: spending approved and not yet paid. It holds budget and is not in the books until it is paid.
- Staff advances, requested, issued and retired
- Petty cash boxes, each with a set amount and a custodian

An approved budget never changes. A revision is a new version, and it cannot cut a category below what is already committed or spent. Whoever requested spending never approves or pays it. Rejected, cancelled and voided spending stays on record.

### Procurement

Buying, with each step held apart by permission.

- Purchase requests with line items
- Approval before going to market
- Quotes compared and one chosen
- Purchase orders
- Goods received against the order
- Supplier invoice, then spending

### Accounting

Double-entry accounting, not a separate system. Paying an expense or receiving a grant payment posts its journal in the same step. If the books refuse it, the payment is refused too, so they cannot disagree.

Ledger

- Chart of accounts
- Journals, with approval
- Recurring journals
- Periods and year end
- Cost centres, projects and funds
- Project posting
- Accounting budgets

Owed and owing

- Invoices and customer payments
- Bills, suppliers and supplier payments
- Aged balances

Cash and assets

- Bank accounts and rules
- Statement reconciliation
- Fixed assets, categories and depreciation
- Currencies and revaluation
- Tax codes and returns

Reports

- Trial balance and general ledger
- Profit and loss, and profit and loss by dimension
- Balance sheet and cash flow
- Financial ratios
- Budget against actual

### Documents

Policies, contracts, certificates and other records, with every version kept. Confidential documents stay limited to the people allowed to see them.

### Administration and security

Who can do what, and the record of what they did.

- Several organizations on one account
- Invitations by one-time link
- Roles and permissions
- Departments and teams
- Audit log
- Two-step sign-in and recovery codes
- Signed-in devices, signed out remotely
- Search and notifications
- Sign-in with email, and with Google when it is configured

Parts an organization can turn off, without deleting anything: Documents, Grants and funders, Procurement, and Accounting. Turning one off hides it. Turning it on again brings it back. While accounting is off, paid spending and grant payments are still posted, so the books stay complete.

## Sharing

The organization decides what its donor sees. Every grant has its own switches, all off until someone turns them on. Donors see only their own grants, even when an organization has several.

What can be shared

- Approved budget, by category, once a budget is approved
- Spending on this grant: paid lines and the committed total
- Project progress: status, percent done and milestones
- Project tasks: number, title, status and dates

What stays with the organization

- Suppliers and receipts
- Who requested or approved
- Staff and the cost of people
- Risks and internal notes
- Who a task is assigned to

## The life of a grant

1. The donor invites the organization, by name and email, as a beneficiary or an implementer. Nothing connects until its administrator accepts.
2. The organization accepts and records the grant: awards, numbered amendments, allocations to projects and a payment schedule, all in its own workspace.
3. The organization chooses what to share: budget, spending, project progress and tasks. Each one stays off until it is turned on.
4. The organization delivers and spends. Tasks move, purchases are approved, and each paid expense posts a journal to the books.
5. The organization submits its reports. Each report has a due date and documents, and the donor sees them once it is submitted.
6. The donor accepts or asks for changes. A revision request sends the report back as owed. A grant closes only when no report is owed.

## Two workspaces

### Organization workspace

Where the organization runs its own work.

Overview: Portfolio, My work.

Projects: Projects, Programmes, Spaces, People, Procurement.

Funding: Grants, Funders. A funder’s contact who is confined to the portal sees only Your grants.

Finance: Approvals, Commitments, Staff advances, Petty cash, Accounting.

Records: Documents.

Administration: Members, Departments and teams, Roles and permissions, Settings, Audit log.

### Donor workspace

Where an organization that funds others follows what those organizations share.

Overview: Dashboard.

Funding: Beneficiaries, Reports, Projects, Tasks.

Administration: Members, Departments and teams, Settings, Audit log.

Totals are kept per currency and never added across currencies. Reports to decide and reports still owed appear across beneficiaries. Payments expected are shown, and overdue items are marked.

## Roles

| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Organization Administrator | Everything, plus approvals, grants, members, roles, settings and the audit log                                                     |
| Project Manager            | Everything on projects, including drafting and submitting budgets and requesting spending; organizes spaces and lists; sees grants |
| Team Member                | View, work tasks, log their own time, request spending                                                                             |
| Viewer                     | View only                                                                                                                          |
| Finance Controller         | View, budgets and costs, people’s costs; approves budgets and spending, records payments; manages funders and grants               |
| Finance Officer            | View, budgets and costs, grants; records payments of approved spending                                                             |

## What guides it

The organization decides. Whoever delivers the work chooses what leaves their workspace, one grant and one switch at a time.

Different hands. Whoever asks for money never approves or pays it. The product enforces that.

History stays. Amendments are numbered, approved budgets do not change, and spending is voided rather than deleted.

Figures that mean something. Committed is not spent, and amounts in different currencies are never added together.

Said plainly. If something is not built, it is marked Planned and listed in one place.

Projects keeps each organization to its own records. A query made without an organization returns nothing. It does not claim a security certification.

## Accounts

Sign up, verify the email, and set up an organization. The person who sets it up is its owner. One account can belong to several organizations.

Sign in with email and password. When Google sign-in is configured, that is offered too. A forgotten password is reset by email. Five failed sign-ins a minute for an address and IP lock it briefly.

Your account holds two-step sign-in with an authenticator app, one-time recovery codes, the devices you are signed in on, and closing the account. Closing is refused while you are the only owner of an active organization.

Members are invited with a one-time link. Someone new sets their name and password there. Someone who already has an account signs in and accepts.
